Read the map as one season

  1. The buyer in the market town signs a price-band contract before planting.
  2. Suitability, water and rainfall say which kebeles can grow it, and when.
  3. The planner sets hectares per cluster A, B and C.
  4. Cooperative stores receive seed and fertilizer ordered back from planting day.
  5. Agents follow the field calendar; satellites flag plots that fall behind.
  6. Harvest lots go to the collection centres to be dried, graded and stored.
  7. Trucks collect before the earth roads close and deliver to the town.
  8. The settlement pays households, and the results feed next season's plan.
