Build roadmap

Phase 1: built one module at a time

The twelve stages describe the whole system. Phase 1 builds its foundation in a fixed order, and each module is finished before the next one starts: first the land, then the water, then where the two meet for irrigation, and finally what to grow there.

Sequence set with the project's academic lead. Each module's output is the next module's input.

Module 1: Agricultural land availability and suitability v0.1 built

Which land may be farmed, and how well would each crop grow there?

First the land's calendar (is it free?), then its skills (is it good?).

Parts

  • v0.1 built1a Land availability: in use, expandable, conditional, excluded
  • v0.1 built1b Land suitability: FAO, AHP and deep learning, S1 to N per crop

Inputs

  • Land cover
  • Elevation and slope
  • Soils
  • Season rainfall and temperature
  • Protected areas, church forests, exclosures
  • Roads and communal grazing

Done when

  • Thresholds agreed with the woreda office and kebele land committees
  • Synthetic layers replaced by the pilot woreda's real layers
  • Cropland, forest and suitability checked against field visits
Hands to the next module: AvailableLand: hectares available and S1 or S2 per crop and cluster
Stage 01

Module 2: Water resource availability mapping v0.1 built

Where can water come from for that land, and how much in each month?

A map of the wells and taps, not just the size of the tank.

Parts

  • v0.1 built2a Monthly water budget for a cluster (river, wells, environmental flow)
  • v0.1 built2b Water source maps: streams and monthly flow, springs, groundwater potential
  • v0.1 built2c Water access per available field: gravity, pumped, shallow wells or harvest only

Inputs

  • AvailableLand from module 1
  • River network and flow records
  • Rainfall and evapotranspiration
  • Geology and recharge for groundwater
  • Existing schemes and water-user associations

Done when

  • Source maps checked with woreda water office records
  • Monthly volumes within agreed environmental-flow limits
  • Every available cell has a water-access class
Hands to the next module: WaterAvailabilityMap: per cell, the reachable sources and the water they can give each month
Stage 02

Module 3: Irrigated land suitability analysis next

Which available land can be irrigated well, by which method?

Good land and water are not enough; they must meet: water has to reach the field at the right height and soil.

Parts

  • next3a Irrigation land factors: slope, soil depth, texture, drainage, salinity
  • planned3b Water access from module 2: source, distance, lift, command area
  • planned3c Classes S1 to N per method: surface, sprinkler, drip

Inputs

  • AvailableLand from module 1
  • WaterAvailabilityMap from module 2
  • Soil physical properties
  • Irrigation method requirements

Done when

  • Classes checked against existing irrigation schemes
  • Command areas agreed with water-user associations
  • Monthly water limits respected in every command area
Hands to the next module: IrrigatedSuitability: S1 to N per method, and irrigable command areas with their water

Module 4: High-value crop production guidance planned

Which high-value crops suit each irrigable area, and how should they be grown?

The menu is written only after we know the kitchen and the ingredients.

Parts

  • planned4a Crop requirements for onion, tomato, pepper, potato and garlic
  • planned4b Crop choice per command area: water per month, margin, market window
  • planned4c Production guides: calendar, inputs, irrigation schedule, post-harvest care

Inputs

  • IrrigatedSuitability from module 3
  • Monthly water from module 2
  • Prices and market windows (stage 05)
  • Agronomic practice from research partners

Done when

  • Guides reviewed by extension and research partners
  • Recommendations tested on demonstration plots
  • Farmers and development agents can use them in the field
Hands to the next module: CropGuide: recommended crops per command area, with calendar, inputs and irrigation schedule

How each module is finished

Domain model first

The words, records and rules are agreed before code: what "available", "water access" or "irrigable" mean, and who decides the thresholds.

Code with tests

Python (PyTorch where learning helps), with tests for every rule and clear error messages when data is missing or misaligned.

Demo, then real data

A synthetic demo shows how the method behaves; the pilot woreda's real layers replace it, with field checks.

A page and a hand-off

Each module gets a page with maps and a calculator, and a defined record that the next module reads.

Phase 1 and the twelve stages

Phase 1 does not replace the chain; it builds the chain's foundation first. Modules 1 and 2 deepen stages 01 and 02, module 3 joins them, and module 4 feeds the planning and field stages. Later phases complete the rest of the chain, from inputs to the market.
